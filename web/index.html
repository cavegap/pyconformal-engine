The story behind the square

A nineteenth-century idea.
A lunar journey today.

The 2015 thesis begins with a mathematical route: stereographic projection takes a hemisphere to a disk; Schwarz–Christoffel mapping provides the next step toward Peirce’s square.

Read the thesis introduction
  1. 1860s

    Schwarz & Christoffel

    Hermann Amandus Schwarz and Elwin Bruno Christoffel independently developed a way to map a half-plane conformally onto a polygon. Their construction is the mathematical foundation explored in the thesis.

    Thesis, §3.4, pp. 48–49
  2. 1879

    Charles Sanders Peirce

    Peirce published A Quincuncial Projection of the Sphere: a world map arranged in a square, with one hemisphere at the center and the other divided among four corners.

    Read Peirce’s original paper
  3. 2015–16

    From the thesis to a continuing question

    Vega Penagos and Olivero Zapata’s thesis proposed further study of Schwarz–Christoffel mapping and its relation to Peirce. Solanilla, Oostra and Yáñez’s 2016 paper supplies a mathematical and computational reference for the projection. Today, this project brings those connections to an interactive lunar atlas.

    Thesis recommendations, p. 59Solanilla, Oostra & Yáñez (2016)
