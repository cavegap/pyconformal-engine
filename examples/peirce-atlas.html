One Moon, four views of Peirce

Northern hemisphere composition

ζ = tan(π/4 − φ/2) exp(i(λ + π/4)); p = i(1 − ζ)/(1 + ζ)
Global square w = (1 + i) S(p)/2; pole-relative scale = (1 + |ζ|²)/√|1 − ζ⁴|

Here φ is latitude and λ is east longitude, in radians in these formulas. For southern latitudes, the corresponding absolute-latitude square is reflected sector by sector before rotation and scaling. The scale formula uses absolute latitude.

Notation, domains and normalization

Keep the same selected point while switching between the hemisphere structure, lunar imagery, local scale and joined tiles. All views use the same normalized projection.

How to explore this example

  1. Select geographically

    Use longitude, latitude, a named crater, pole or cut button. The map is a readout: clicking it does not select a point.

  2. Switch the view

    Keep the same geographic point P while comparing structure, imagery, relative scale and joined tiles. Checkboxes show or hide layers.

  3. Read the legend

    The cyan diamond is the equator; cyan graticule lines are parallels and gold ones are meridians. Gold dots identify crater centers; the white ring selects P.

The four S corners represent one south pole. Joined tiles repeat the same Moon. The scale colors mean relative length enlargement, not elevation or image brightness.

Projection, cuts and interpretation

For φ ≥ 0: ζ = tan(π/4 − φ/2)exp[i(λ + π/4)], p = i(1 − ζ)/(1 + ζ), s = 1 + i − (2i/κ)RF(p + 1,p,p − 1), w = (1 + i)s/2. Here κ = RF(0,1,2), and angles in the formulas are radians. Southern sectors are reflected across the corresponding equatorial side before the final rotation and scaling.

The cyan diamond is the equator. N is at the center; the four S labels represent one south pole. Cyan diamonds at the edge midpoints are exceptional equatorial cut points. In tile mode, neighbors alternate 180° turns and the same P is repeated in each copy. Repetition adds no geographic coverage. Graticule colors distinguish parallels (cyan) and meridians (gold), not height or composition.

Relative length scale = (1 + |ζ|²)/√|1 − ζ⁴| using |φ| in ζ; it equals one at either pole. The single tile uses dimensionless coordinates in [−1,1]². Crater centers are illustrative references. This global atlas does not use the approximate archival tracing registration.
