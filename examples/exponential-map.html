The exponential map w = ez

What you can explore

Explain periodicity and local invertibility. Compare a straight grid with its circular image. Use points separated by one period to explain why a nonzero derivative does not imply global injectivity.

Drag P or use the sliders. A horizontal line maps to a segment of a ray. A vertical line maps to a circle over one full turn.

Exponential map

f(x + iy) = exp(x)(cos y + i sin y); f′(z) = exp z
f(z + 2πi) = f(z); local length scale = exp(Re z)

The derivative never vanishes. The map is locally conformal everywhere, and becomes one-to-one when restricted to a suitable open horizontal strip.

Notation, domains and normalization

Formula and object example

w = exp(x + iy) = eˣ(cos y + i sin y); f′(z) = exp z never vanishes. Local angles are preserved, but points separated by 2πi have the same image. Horizontal lines become rays and vertical lines become circles. See a logarithm recover a recognizable house from an exponential deformation.

How to explore this example

  1. Move the source

    Change x and y using the source plot or sliders. The y slider is displayed in degrees; source coordinates use radians.

  2. Match the colors

    The brown horizontal line becomes a ray segment. The green vertical line becomes a circle. Their intersection is P → P′.

  3. Compare the edges

    The −180°/+180° button changes y by 2π. The source point moves while its image stays at the same location.

The exponential is locally conformal but periodic. Small image circles for negative x are the expected contraction, not a missing curve.
