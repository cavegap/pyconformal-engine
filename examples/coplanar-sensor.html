Coplanar capacitive sensor

Change the gap and see how two finite-width electrodes couple through a uniform dielectric.

Ideal two-strip capacitance

k = g/(g + 2wt); k′ = √(1 − k²)
C′ = ε0 εr K(k′)/K(k); C ≈ L C′

Here g is the full gap, wₜ is the width of each strip, and L is its physical length in meters. K uses the same modulus convention as the waveguide formula; the geometrical definitions of k differ.

Where this helps · capacitive coupling and sensing

Use this model to compare the coupling between two coplanar electrodes, study a spacing change, or estimate the response to a uniform change in dielectric permittivity. At fixed geometry, doubling εᵣ doubles C. The double-gap comparison shows the effect of moving the strips apart.

The equations above use the complete elliptic integral of the first kind, with its modulus convention stated explicitly.

F^{\prime}(\zeta)=\frac{A}{\sqrt{(\zeta-1)(\zeta+1)(\zeta-k)(\zeta+k)}}
\zeta=\frac{x+iy}{g/2+w_{\mathrm{t}}}

The four square-root factors come from the SC geometry. The model fixes the normalization and phase. The drawn line density is illustrative and does not measure field magnitude.

A material sample or nearby object usually occupies only part of the field. That sensing application needs a layered or spatial dielectric model and calibration; this uniform model does not predict that response directly. Derivation and independent validation · Compare the 50 Ω guide
