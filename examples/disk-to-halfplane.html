From disk to half-plane

What you can explore

Choose a simpler domain for a problem. Track how a bounded disk becomes an unbounded half-plane. Read the boundary correspondence and pole before using the map in another calculation.

w = i(1 − z)/(1 + z)

Drag P or use the sliders. Move to the unit circle to follow its image along the real axis.

Cayley map

f(z) = i(1 − z)/(1 + z); f′(z) = −2i/(1 + z)²
Im f(z) = (1 − |z|²)/|1 + z|² > 0 for |z| < 1

The open disk maps to the upper half-plane. The boundary point −1 is a pole, so its image is interpreted on the extended complex plane.

Notation, domains and normalization

Formula and reference points

w = i(1 − z)/(1 + z), with Im w = (1 − |z|²)/|1 + z|². Thus the open unit disk maps into the upper half-plane. Reference points: 0 → i, i → 1, −i → −1, 1 → 0; −1 is the pole. Compare an interior point, a boundary point and the pole with the controls.

How to explore this example

  1. Choose a point

    Click or drag in the disk. Radius and direction give an alternative way to move P.

  2. Follow P → P′

    Interior points go above the real axis. Use Move to boundary to see boundary points map onto that axis.

  3. Try the pole

    Move to pole sends P to −1. Its image is infinity, so there is no finite image marker.

Near the pole, P′ may lie beyond the plotted window. The location message distinguishes an off-screen finite point from infinity.
