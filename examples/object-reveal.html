A logarithm reveals an object

What you can explore

Understand what an inverse branch recovers. Start with a deliberately deformed house and follow its recovery by Log. Identify the domain restriction that makes this constructed inverse work.

Principal logarithm on the source domain

f(z) = Log z; f′(z) = 1/z; Re z > 0

The constructed source lies in the right half-plane, so the principal branch is analytic and one-to-one there. The chosen house fits inside a single branch strip.

Notation, domains and normalization

The curved source becomes a house with straight walls and a roof under w = Log z. Follow P around the colored contour: corresponding vertices A–E stay visible in both views.

How to explore this example

  1. Select a position

    Click near the contour in either view, or move Position of P. This selects an existing sample; it does not drag a vertex.

  2. Follow the labels

    P is on the deformed contour and P′ on the recovered house. The edge colors and A–E labels identify corresponding pieces.

  3. Read the construction

    The house was chosen first and deformed with exp. Applying the principal logarithm recovers that designed house.

This example illustrates an inverse map on a stated branch; it does not reconstruct an unknown object from incomplete information.

Controls

How it was constructed: start with a house in the w-plane, generate the source with z = exp w, then recover it with Log z. This is an inverse-designed example, not recovery of hidden information from an unknown object.

Here |Im w| ≤ 1 < π/2, so the source lies in Re z > 0. The principal logarithm is one-to-one and conformal there, with f′(z) = 1/z. Local angles are preserved; curvature and lengths can change. The largest recovered straight lines need not have been straight in the source.
